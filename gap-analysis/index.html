Gap Analysis

A live gap analysis dashboard tracking the molecular barcode coverage of Nigerian coastal and marine biodiversity across all major taxonomic groups. Updated as new sequences are generated and deposited.

Nigeria's EEZ covers ~210,000 km² of the Gulf of Guinea. A major limitation for eDNA-based biodiversity monitoring is the absence of regionally representative reference sequences — without them, even a perfect metabarcoding protocol cannot reliably assign Nigerian specimens. This page explains the gap, how coverage is classified, and how the library is addressing it.

NEW IN v21 Methodology update — marker-specific WA thresholds Released April 2026
★ Classification rule rewritten
The v20 "WA ≥50% of global NCBI" ratio is retired. Classification now uses marker-specific absolute WA record thresholds — COI ≥5, 12S ≥3, 18S ≥1, 28S ≥1, rbcL ≥3 — chosen to reflect the intraspecific variation needed for reliable identification with each marker.
⚠ Scope change — WA-absent now means absent
A species with global records but zero West African records is now classified NCBI-absent for the regional reference library. Global coverage no longer rescues regional gaps. This affects Tursiops truncatus, Chelonia mydas, Dermochelys coriacea, Lepidochelys olivacea, Carcharhinus leucas, Rhizoprionodon acutus, Parapenaeus longirostris, and others.
Δ Reclassification summary
Three overall-status changes under the new rule: Sardinella maderensis present → partial; Rhizoprionodon acutus and Penaeus notialis partial → absent. Several NCBI-level statuses corrected upward (P. elongatus, Sphyrna lewini) or downward (all turtles, cetaceans, and deep-water shrimps with zero WA records).
⚡ UI updates
Decision-tree flowchart redrawn with a three-stage logic (any WA? → meets threshold? → both databases?) and a marker-threshold legend. All PRESENT/PARTIAL/ABSENT definition cards, Statistics page copy, coverage legend, and species-level notes updated to match. Stale v18/v20 references corrected.
🔬 Scientific rationale
The 50% ratio was mathematically unstable for data-rich species (a fish with 80/400 WA records failed despite operational sufficiency, while a species with 2/2 passed). Absolute marker-specific thresholds align classification with the real question: "can we reliably identify a Nigerian specimen from existing reference data?" Global counts are retained as context but no longer classify.
🛡 Audit trail
Species entries previously flagged as "present" with ratio-inflated status (e.g. Sardinella maderensis at 2/2 = 100%) have been reviewed and corrected. Critical gaps, well-covered, and partial coverage lists reconciled. Priority flags adjusted where overall status changed.

Why coverage matters

Every species in the "absent" column could represent a false negative in any eDNA metabarcoding study conducted in Nigerian waters. The animal or plant may be present, its DNA may be in your sample, and your PCR may amplify it perfectly — but without a matching reference barcode, your analysis software returns nothing. This dashboard makes the scale of that problem visible and actionable.

Gulf of Guinea endemics — priority targets

Pteroscion peli (boe drum), Lutjanus goreensis (Gorean snapper), Scomberomorus tritor (West African Spanish mackerel), and Strombus latus (West African stromb) are among several species with zero sequences anywhere in global databases. Each represents a publishable first-barcode contribution. Tympanotonos fuscatus — the most commercially important macroinvertebrate in the Niger Delta — has partial global coverage but zero Nigeria-specific records.

What is in this database

Taxonomic groups covered

  • Fish (estuarine + coastal) — 16 priority species, 12S rRNA marker
  • Elasmobranchs — sharks and rays of the Nigerian coast
  • Marine pelagic fish — tuna, mackerel, swordfish, billfish
  • Marine demersal fish — offshore snappers, groupers, dentex
  • Coral reef and hard substrate — corals, sea urchins, holothurians
  • Macrobenthos — Tympanotonos, Pachymelania, bivalves, polychaetes, mangrove crabs
  • Nematodes — 25 genera across 8 orders, 18S + 28S
  • Meiofauna — copepods, ostracods, foraminifera
  • Seagrass & macroalgae — including Sargassum bloom species
  • Marine mammals — including African manatee, Atlantic humpback dolphin
  • Sea turtles — all three nesting species

How to use this database

The Database page shows every species in one searchable, sortable table with filters by group, marker, status, and priority. Use the search bar to find any species instantly.

The Statistics page shows live KPI cards, the Top Priority Sequencing Targets leaderboard, the IUCN × coverage crosstab, and the classification decision-tree flowchart.

All data can be exported as CSV from the Database and Statistics pages for use in your own analysis or grant proposal.

The regional gap — why Nigeria?

eDNA metabarcoding is increasingly used for fisheries stock assessment, MPA monitoring, and invasive species detection. Its accuracy depends entirely on the quality and regional representativeness of the reference database. Global databases like NCBI Nucleotide and BOLD Systems are disproportionately populated by specimens from North America, Europe, and East Asia — West African coastal taxa are dramatically underrepresented. A sequence identified as Penaeus notialis (the most commercially important shrimp in Nigeria) may never be correctly assigned in a Nigerian water sample, because the 45 global COI records come from Mediterranean and Atlantic fisheries, not West Africa.

This library directly addresses that gap by: (1) systematically identifying which species are covered and which are not, using the regional classification rule described above; (2) generating new Nigerian voucher sequences and depositing them in BOLD under project code NGMBL; and (3) publishing the reference framework as a citable data paper to catalyse parallel efforts across the Gulf of Guinea.
