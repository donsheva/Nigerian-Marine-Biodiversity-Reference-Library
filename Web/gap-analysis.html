Coastal fish context

The Niger Delta and Gulf of Guinea coastal zone supports one of Africa's largest small-scale fisheries, with estimated annual artisanal landings exceeding 700,000 tonnes (FAO, 2020). Key species include Ethmalosa fimbriata (bonga shad), which accounts for up to 40% of artisanal catch by weight in Lagos Lagoon (Lawal-Are & Akinjogunla, 2012), yet has no complete 12S barcode from Nigerian waters in any global database.

Reference library gap — West Africa

A global review of eDNA metabarcoding studies found that Sub-Saharan Africa has the lowest regional representation in molecular reference databases of any tropical region (Manaf et al., 2025, Molecular Ecology Resources). False negatives in eDNA surveys are therefore disproportionately common in West African biodiversity assessments, rendering standard metabarcoding protocols unreliable without a region-specific library.

MiFish 12S — the global standard

The MiFish primer set (Miya et al., 2015, Royal Society Open Science) targets a ~170bp region of the mitochondrial 12S rRNA gene and can detect over 230 fish species simultaneously from a single 2L water sample. It is now the most widely adopted fish eDNA marker globally, with reference sequences available for over 7,000 species — but fewer than 50 from the Gulf of Guinea specifically.

Gulf of Guinea Marine Ecosystems

Nigeria's EEZ covers approximately 210,000 km² of the Gulf of Guinea, one of the most productive marine ecosystems in the Atlantic. The Gulf of Guinea is characterised by a seasonal upwelling system that drives exceptionally high primary productivity, supporting both coastal small-scale fisheries and offshore industrial trawl and purse-seine operations (Cury & Roy, 1989, Canadian Journal of Fisheries and Aquatic Sciences). This tab covers truly marine species: offshore fish, reef taxa, marine invertebrates, seagrass, macroalgae, and offshore mammals. Primary markers: 12S rRNA (MiFish-U/E) for fish; COI (Leray-XT) for invertebrates and mammals; rbcL for plants and algae.

Gulf of Guinea endemics

Pteroscion peli, Lutjanus goreensis, and Scomberomorus tritor are among several species whose entire global range lies within or near the Gulf of Guinea. None have been barcoded from Nigerian waters. First-barcode records from this region would represent genuinely novel contributions to global sequence databases (Hebert et al., 2003, Proc. Royal Soc. B).

Sargassum inundations

Since 2011, massive Sargassum inundations have struck Nigerian coastal waters annually, impacting fisheries and coastal tourism (Wang et al., 2019, Science). eDNA-based monitoring using rbcL reference sequences enables source tracking and early warning detection before physical landfall.

Sargassum — emerging monitoring priority
Since 2011, massive Sargassum inundations have increasingly impacted Nigerian coastal waters, smothering nearshore fish nurseries and fouling fishing gear. Two species — S. natans and S. fluitans — dominate these blooms. Having rbcL reference sequences from the Gulf of Guinea allows eDNA-based early detection and source tracking of bloom events, directly relevant to NIOMR's monitoring mandate.
Gulf of Guinea endemics — highest scientific value
Several species in this list are endemic or near-endemic to the Gulf of Guinea — found nowhere else in the world. Pteroscion peli (boe drum), Lutjanus goreensis (Gorean snapper), and Scomberomorus tritor (West African Spanish mackerel) are examples with zero or near-zero sequences. These are the highest-priority targets because their barcodes do not exist anywhere — not just in Nigeria.

Script 1 — Fish & vertebrates (marker: 12S / COI)
Queries NCBI for all fish, elasmobranchs, crustaceans, mammals, and turtles. Saves ncbi_gap_report.csv. Includes automatic retry on server errors.
# ncbi_query_vertebrates.py
from Bio import Entrez
import pandas as pd, time

Entrez.email = "you@email.com"  # replace with your real email

TAXA = [
    ("Ethmalosa fimbriata",           "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Sardinella maderensis",          "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Pseudotolithus senegalensis",    "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Pseudotolithus elongatus",       "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Cynoglossus senegalensis",       "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Galeoides decadactylus",         "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Polydactylus quadrifilis",       "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Lutjanus agennes",               "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Arius latiscutatus",             "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Brachydeuterus auritus",         "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Elops lacerta",                  "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Mugil cephalus",                 "Fish",         "12S"),
    ("Rhizoprionodon acutus",          "Elasmobranch", "12S"),
    ("Rhinobatos rhinobatos",          "Elasmobranch", "12S"),
    ("Carcharhinus leucas",            "Elasmobranch", "12S"),
    ("Trichechus senegalensis",        "Mammal",       "COI"),
    ("Sousa teuszii",                  "Mammal",       "COI"),
    ("Penaeus notialis",               "Crustacean",   "COI"),
    ("Callinectes amnicola",           "Crustacean",   "COI"),
    ("Chelonia mydas",                 "Turtle",       "12S"),
    ("Dermochelys coriacea",           "Turtle",       "12S"),
]

def query(term, retries=3):
    for i in range(retries):
        try:
            h = Entrez.esearch(db="nucleotide", term=term)
            r = Entrez.read(h); h.close()
            return int(r["Count"])
        except: time.sleep(5)
    return -1

WA = ["Nigeria","Ghana","Cameroon","Benin","Togo","Senegal","Guinea","Ivory Coast","Liberia"]
records = []
for sp, group, marker in TAXA:
    w = query(f'"{sp}"[Organism] AND {marker}[Title] AND ({" OR ".join(f"{c}[Country]" for c in WA)})')
    st = "absent" if g==0 else "partial" if w==0 else "present"
    print(f"  {sp:<45} global:{g:>5}  W.Africa:{w:>4}  [{st}]")
    records.append({"species":sp,"group":group,"marker":marker,
                    "global_count":g,"wa_count":w,"status":st})
    time.sleep(0.5)

pd.DataFrame(records).to_csv("ncbi_gap_vertebrates.csv", index=False)
print("Saved: ncbi_gap_vertebrates.csv")

    ("Plectus",            "Plectida",      "other"),
]

WA  = ["Nigeria","Ghana","Cameroon","Benin","Togo","Senegal","Guinea","Ivory Coast","Liberia"]
NIG = ["Nigeria","Niger Delta","Lagos","Bayelsa","Delta State"]

def query(term, retries=3):
    for i in range(retries):
        try:
            h = Entrez.esearch(db="nucleotide", term=term)
            r = Entrez.read(h); h.close()
            return int(r["Count"])
        except: time.sleep(5)
    return -1

print(f"{'Genus':<22} {'Order':<18} {'Gl 18S':>7} {'WA 18S':>7} {'NG 18S':>7} {'Gl 28S':>7} {'NG 28S':>7}")

for genus, order, response in GENERA:
    row = {"genus":genus, "order":order, "pollution_response":response}
    for m in ["18S","28S"]:
        row[f"global_{m}"] = query(f'"{genus}"[Organism] AND {m}[Title]'); time.sleep(0.4)

    print(f"  {genus:<20} {order:<18} {row['global_18S']:>7} {row['wa_18S']:>7} {row['ng_18S']:>7} {row['global_28S']:>7} {row['ng_28S']:>7}  {flag}")

Script 3 — BOLD query (Gulf of Guinea, all groups)
Downloads barcodes from BOLD Systems for Nigeria and neighbouring countries. Saves bold_gulf_of_guinea.fasta for immediate use in local BLAST searches.
# bold_query.py
import requests, time

BASE   = "http://www.boldsystems.org/index.php/API_Public/combined"
GROUPS = ["Actinopterygii","Elasmobranchii","Malacostraca",
          "Nematoda","Cetacea","Testudines","Trichechus"]
GEOS   = ["Nigeria","Ghana","Cameroon","Benin","Togo","Senegal","Guinea","Ivory Coast","Liberia"]

all_records = {}
for taxon in GROUPS:
    for geo in GEOS:
        try:
            r = requests.get(BASE, params={"taxon":taxon,"geo":geo,"format":"json"}, timeout=30)
            if r.status_code==200 and r.text.strip():
                recs = r.json().get("bold_records",{})
                all_records.update(recs)
                print(f"  {taxon}/{geo}: {len(recs)} records")
        except Exception as e: print(f"  Error {taxon}/{geo}: {e}")
        time.sleep(0.8)

with open("bold_gulf_of_guinea.fasta","w") as f:
    for rid, rec in all_records.items():
        seq = rec.get("sequences",{}).get("sequence","")
        sp  = rec.get("taxonomy",{}).get("species",{}).get("taxon",{}).get("name","unknown")
        geo = rec.get("collection_event",{}).get("country","")
        if seq:
            f.write(f">{rid}|{sp}|{geo}\n{seq}\n")

print(f"\nTotal: {len(all_records)} records")
print("Saved: bold_gulf_of_guinea.fasta")
input("Press Enter to close...")
Script 4 — Build and update your local library
After Sanger sequencing your voucher specimens, use this to add sequences and generate QIIME2-compatible taxonomy files. Works for both 12S (fish) and 18S (nematodes).
# build_library.py
from Bio import SeqIO
from Bio.SeqRecord import SeqRecord
from Bio.Seq import Seq
import os, csv

YOUR_SEQUENCES = [
    # Fish example (12S marker)
    { "id":"NGA_2025_001", "species":"Ethmalosa fimbriata",
      "marker":"12S", "location":"Lagos Lagoon", "lat":6.453, "lon":3.396,
      "date":"2025-03-15", "voucher":"NIOMR-LOS-001",
      "sequence":"ACGTACGT..." },
    # Nematode example (18S marker)
    { "id":"NGA_2025_050", "species":"Terschellingia sp.",
      "marker":"18S", "location":"Bonny River, Niger Delta", "lat":4.443, "lon":7.152,
      "date":"2025-04-10", "voucher":"NIOMR-ND-050",
      "sequence":"ACGTACGT..." },
]

for marker in ["12S","18S","28S","COI"]:
    os.makedirs(f"nigeria_reflib/{marker}", exist_ok=True)

records_by_marker = {}
for s in YOUR_SEQUENCES:
    m = s["marker"]
    if s["sequence"] == "ACGTACGT...": continue
    rec = SeqRecord(Seq(s["sequence"]), id=s["id"],
                    description=f"{s['species']}|{m}|{s['location']}|{s['lat']},{s['lon']}|{s['voucher']}")
    records_by_marker.setdefault(m, []).append((rec, s))

for marker, items in records_by_marker.items():
    recs = [i[0] for i in items]
    metas= [i[1] for i in items]
    SeqIO.write(recs, f"nigeria_reflib/{marker}/nigeria_{marker}.fasta","fasta")
    with open(f"nigeria_reflib/{marker}/taxonomy.tsv","w",newline="") as f:
        w = csv.writer(f, delimiter="\t")
        w.writerow(["Feature ID","Taxon","Marker","Country"])
        for s in metas:
            w.writerow([s["id"], s["species"], marker, "Nigeria"])
    print(f"  {marker}: {len(recs)} sequences → nigeria_reflib/{marker}/")

print("Done. Files ready for QIIME2 or BLAST.")
input("Press Enter to close...")

Two parallel tracks — vertebrates/fish (12S, fish market vouchers) and nematodes (18S/28S, sediment cores). They share the same deposit and analysis infrastructure.

1
Install Python tools — today, 30 minutes
pip install biopython pandas requests openpyxl — then run Scripts 1 and 2 to produce your gap CSVs. No fieldwork needed yet.
Download Python NCBI Nucleotide SILVA 18S database
2
Fish market vouchers — vertebrate track
Lagos fish markets (Mile 12, Oyingbo, Badore) land 40–60 species daily. Bring a cooler, scissors, 2ml ethanol-filled tubes, and a smartphone. Take one fin clip per fish, photograph it first. A single morning visit yields 20–30 tissue samples covering your highest-priority gap species with zero fieldwork permits required.
3
Sediment core collection — nematode track
Collect 10cm push cores from intertidal/subtidal sites across your pollution gradient — clean reference site, moderately impacted, heavily impacted (oil spill history). Split each core: half in 4% formalin for morphological ID under microscope, half frozen at −20°C for 18S eDNA extraction. Three replicate cores per site minimum.
NeMys nematode register WoRMS taxonomy
4
Sanger sequencing — both tracks
Fish tissues → PCR with MiFish-U/E primers (12S) → Sanger sequencing at Macrogen (~$5/sample). Nematode single specimens → photograph under microscope → pick into 5µl water → lyse → PCR with NF1/18Sr2b primers (18S V1V2) → Sanger. Turnaround 3–5 days from Macrogen Korea.
Macrogen sequencing
5
Deposit sequences — BOLD + NCBI + SILVA
Create a BOLD project "NGCW" (Nigeria Coastal Waters). Upload fish sequences tagged with voucher IDs and GPS coordinates. For nematodes, also submit to SILVA's SINA aligner — SILVA is more complete than NCBI for 18S and is the primary database used by QIIME2 classifiers. Use Script 4 to organise locally before upload.
Create BOLD project SILVA SINA aligner NCBI submission
6
Publish — data paper + pollution baseline study
Two publishable outputs emerge naturally: (1) a data paper describing the reference library (Scientific Data, Biodiversity Data Journal) — fundable and citable; (2) a validation paper comparing eDNA metabarcoding nematode communities vs morphological identification across a Niger Delta pollution gradient, with N:C ratio analysis. The second paper is the flagship contribution.
